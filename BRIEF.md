# Run flexible work before deadlines with low energy and carbon cost

Your site processes preemptible work on a half-hour clock. Decide how many integer
work units to run from each currently available job. Every unit consumes 1 kWh.
Jobs arrive over time, and unfinished units expire at their exclusive deadline.
The objective is to complete work while reducing the published joint cost:
**electricity GBP + £1 per kg of modeled CO2**.

There are 40 decisions, representing 04:00–24:00 of a replay day. An initial eight
half-hour carbon measurements provide warm-up history. You know all electricity
tariffs, current capacity and released jobs. You do **not** know current-interval
or future actual carbon, nor unreleased jobs. Carbon reports become available according to their delivery timestamps. These historical carbon estimates support a simulated scheduling exercise;
the score is not evidence of actual emissions avoided.

The operations model is simulated. Each episode has 22 jobs, each with 3–8 units.
Three jobs release at slot 0; the others release at multiples of four from slot
0 through 28. Deadlines are 6–16 slots after release, clipped at slot 40. Case
construction retains workloads that a deadline-first full-service schedule can
complete. Ordinary capacity is four units per slot. Work from the same job may
run in parallel and may be split across slots. There are no ramp, startup,
storage or job-switching costs. There is no benefit to running nonexistent work.

## Interface

Implement `solution.py:decide(observation, state)` returning
`{"action":{"dispatch":{"w00":2}},"state":...}`. State is your JSON memory and
resets each episode. The observation includes:

- `slot`, `horizon_slots`, `capacity_units`, `unit_kwh`, `slot_hours`;
- `jobs`: released, unfinished, unexpired jobs with `id`, `release`, exclusive
  `deadline`, original `units` and `remaining_units`;
- `tariff_gbp_per_kwh`: the known 40-slot tariff schedule;
- `forecast`: rows for current and remaining slots, each with `slot`, predicted
  `carbon_g_per_kwh` and a heuristic `uncertainty_g_per_kwh`;
- `history`: observed carbon values only; negative slots are warm-up history;
- `previous_dispatch`: previous action, validity and work units; carbon reports are separate;
- `completed_units`, `carbon_price_gbp_per_kg`, `done`.

Return only a `dispatch` map inside the action. Values must be nonnegative
integers (booleans/fractions are invalid). Total allocation must fit current
capacity, and each job must be available and have enough remaining work. Empty
allocation is allowed. An invalid action is rejected atomically, runs no work,
and consumes its slot. There is no silent clipping or substitution.

Forecasts are deliberately imperfect **simulated causal forecasts**. They extrapolate the latest measured carbon with
a clipped trend from the previous four measurements, a damping factor and a
fixed illustrative diurnal curve. The horizon error estimate grows from 16 to
about 37 g/kWh and is a heuristic, not a calibrated confidence interval. Historical
forecast errors are visible in practice traces. Replanning, buffers, deadline
heuristics and learning from observed residuals are all legitimate approaches.
The eight initial observations and all subsequently observed measurements are
available to your own predictor.

## Score: one domain-specific rubric, 100 points

Let `D` be total requested units; `q = completed_units / D`; `J` be realized joint
GBP cost; and `J*` be the minimum joint GBP cost of a feasible full-service
hindsight schedule under the same actual carbon, arrivals, deadlines and capacity.
The benchmark has future knowledge solely for grading. It is never supplied to
your policy. Each case has a verified feasible full-service schedule.

`r = max(0, J - q * J*) / J*`

`work_completed = 70 * q`

`joint_efficiency = 30 * q * exp(-r / 0.20)`

`score = work_completed + joint_efficiency`

Joint regret compares one feasible combined objective, rather than independently
incompatible best-price and best-carbon schedules. Completing everything at `J*`
earns 100. A causal policy may not achieve that hindsight ceiling. Doing nothing
earns zero, and partial service cannot earn more than `100*q`, even if the work
chosen is cheap. Missing work and carbon/price tradeoffs therefore matter
continuously. Case scores are averaged equally within each condition. See common
event rules for nomination, stage weights and feedback timing. Tariff and carbon
are reported separately as diagnostics, with no extra marks for either.

## Practice and six-hour scope

`training.json` and `practice.json` contain separate complete public replay traces generated by the
provided deadline-first starter, with realized outcomes and score components.
These are examples, not optimal target actions. Their replay days are
disjoint from validation and finals. `local_check.py` replays the public exogenous carbon and job outcomes with
your own policy, rebuilding remaining work and prior action feedback. It reports
public practice scores using the supplied practice benchmark answers, which are
never passed to `decide`. Run `python local_check.py solution.py`; after release,
add `adaptation.json` as the second argument to replay changed examples. This
small public replay tool contains no held-out data or case generator.

A practical six-hour route is: understand and validate the starter (hour 1),
inspect forecast error and workload pressure (hour 2), implement a deadline-aware
heuristic or rolling optimizer (hours 3–4), compare policies on public evidence
and validation feedback (hour 5), and test the announced change with adaptation
examples and nominate your final policy (hour 6). Setup and dependencies can be
prepared before the event. No model account, API or online data access is needed.
